Buy a full standing desk if you have the space, plan to switch between sitting and standing every day, and want the best ergonomics in both positions. Buy a desk converter if you rent, share a desk, need to keep your current furniture, or just want to find out whether you'll actually stand before spending more. A converter is the easier, lower-commitment option; a standing desk is the better long-term setup for most people who work at a desk full time.

## First, a reality check about standing

Standing all day isn't the goal. Standing for hours brings its own problems: tired legs, sore feet and lower back fatigue. What helps is *changing position* regularly. A common approach is to alternate between sitting and standing every 30 to 60 minutes, and to take short walking or stretching breaks too.

So the real question isn't "which lets me stand?" It's "which will I actually use to switch positions many times a day?" The easier switching is, the more likely you'll do it.

## Try it for free before you buy

If you've never worked standing up, test the habit first:

- **Use a kitchen counter or high dresser** for your laptop for an hour or two a day.
- **Stack sturdy boxes or books** on your desk to raise a laptop and an external keyboard to standing elbow height.
- **Take calls standing up.** It's an easy way to build the habit with no equipment at all.

If after a week or two you're standing regularly and wish it were easier, that's a good sign an adjustable setup will be worth it. If you quickly stop, a converter (or nothing) may be the smarter spend.

## What each option is

### Standing desk

A full desk with an adjustable frame. Most use electric motors in the legs and a control panel with memory presets, so you press a button to go from sitting to standing height. Some cheaper models use a hand crank. The whole desktop moves, so everything on it (monitor, keyboard, lamp, papers) comes along.

### Desk converter

A platform that sits on top of your existing desk and lifts your monitor and keyboard up when you want to stand. Most use gas springs or a spring-assisted mechanism that you lift and lower by hand; some are electric. Many have two tiers: a top surface for the monitor and a lower tray for the keyboard and mouse.

## Where a standing desk wins

### Better ergonomics sitting and standing

With a standing desk, your keyboard sits directly on the desktop at both heights. That makes it straightforward to set the surface at elbow height (elbows around 90 degrees, shoulders relaxed) whether you're sitting or standing. With a monitor arm or riser, you can then set the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away.

### More usable space

You keep your whole work surface: room for notes, a second monitor, a desk lamp and a coffee mug, all at the right height.

### Switching is effortless

An electric desk with memory presets turns switching into a single button press. That matters more than it sounds, because less friction means you'll change positions more often.

### What to look for

- **Height range that fits you.** Check the minimum height (important if you're shorter, so the desk isn't too high when seated) and the maximum height (important if you're tall). Your standing desk height should be close to your elbow height while standing in the shoes you usually wear.
- **Dual motors** if you have a heavy setup or a large desktop. They tend to lift more smoothly and carry more weight than single-motor frames.
- **A stable frame.** Look for sturdy crossbars and feet; wobble tends to get worse at full standing height.
- **Weight capacity** comfortably above the weight of everything on the desk.
- **Memory presets and anti-collision** on electric models.

## Where a desk converter wins

### Keeps your current desk

If you like your desk, can't replace it (rented or shared office, built-in furniture), or already own a solid desk, a converter adds standing without starting over.

### Lower commitment

A converter is usually cheaper and much easier to return, move or sell. It's a sensible way to find out whether you'll actually stand before investing in a full desk.

### Little or no assembly

Most converters come nearly ready to use. That's helpful if you don't have the tools, time or help to assemble a heavy desk frame.

### The trade-offs to know about

- **Seated keyboard height.** Even at its lowest, a converter adds some height above your desk. If your desk is already at a normal height, your keyboard may end up too high when you sit, which pushes your shoulders up. Look for a model whose keyboard tray sits very low when collapsed, or plan to raise your chair and add a footrest.
- **Monitor and keyboard move together.** On many converters, the gap between the keyboard tray and the monitor surface is fixed. If you're tall, the monitor may end up too low when standing. A monitor arm or riser on the top tier can help.
- **Depth.** Converters take up desk depth and some extend toward you as they rise. Measure your desk depth and check the converter's footprint and how far it moves forward.
- **Stability.** Converters, especially at full height with a heavy monitor or two, can bounce while you type. Check weight capacity and look for a wide, solid base.

## How to measure before you buy

1. **Standing elbow height:** stand in your usual shoes, relax your shoulders, bend your elbows to about 90 degrees and measure from the floor to the underside of your forearm. Your keyboard surface should be close to this height.
2. **Seated elbow height:** do the same while sitting with feet flat and your chair adjusted.
3. **For a standing desk:** check that both measurements fall inside the desk's height range (allowing for the thickness of the desktop).
4. **For a converter:** subtract your desk height from your standing elbow height. The converter's keyboard tray must be able to reach that much lift. Then check that its lowest keyboard position doesn't push your seated keyboard height too high.
5. **Desk depth and wall clearance:** make sure the converter fits with room to spare, and that a standing desk has space to move without hitting windowsills, shelves or a wall.

## Common mistakes

- **Standing too long, too soon.** Build up gradually and alternate positions.
- **Ignoring seated ergonomics.** You'll probably still sit for more of the day than you stand, so the seated position has to work well.
- **Forgetting about your feet.** Hard floors get uncomfortable fast. Supportive shoes or a cushioned mat make standing much easier.
- **Buying the cheapest frame available.** A wobbly desk or bouncy converter is frustrating enough that people stop using it.
- **Not checking the height range.** Very short and very tall people are the most likely to find a model doesn't adjust far enough.

## Who should pick which

**Choose a standing desk if you:**

- Work at a desk most of the day, most days of the week
- Have room for a dedicated desk and can replace your current one
- Use a large or multi-monitor setup
- Want the best ergonomics sitting and standing
- Know you'll use it, perhaps because you've already been standing with a makeshift setup

**Choose a desk converter if you:**

- Rent, move often or work in a shared or office space
- Have a desk you want to keep
- Want to try standing without a big commitment
- Have a single monitor or a laptop setup
- Don't want to assemble a heavy desk

## Don't forget the extras

Whichever you choose, a few small things make standing more comfortable. A cushioned mat reduces foot and leg fatigue on hard floors; you can [[anti-fatigue-mats|browse anti-fatigue mats]] if you don't already have a thick rug or supportive shoes. A monitor arm makes it much easier to get screen height right in both positions.

If you notice persistent pain in your back, legs or feet, or any numbness or tingling, stop and get it checked by a doctor or physiotherapist.

## Bottom line

A standing desk is the better long-term setup if you have the space and know you'll use it. A desk converter is the smarter choice if you need to keep your current desk, move often or want to test the habit first. Either way, measure your elbow heights before you buy, alternate between sitting and standing, and set up your seated position as carefully as your standing one.
