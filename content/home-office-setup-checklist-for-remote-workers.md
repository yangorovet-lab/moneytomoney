Use this checklist to set up (or audit) a home office in about an hour. It runs in the order that matters most: where you work, then your chair, desk, and screen, then input devices, lighting, calls, tech, and finally the habits that keep a good setup working. Check each item against your current space, note what's missing, and try the free fix listed for each item before buying anything. Many setups need only two or three purchases, if any.

## 1. Location

Where you put your desk shapes almost everything else.

- **A dedicated spot**, even a small one, that you don't have to set up and pack away each day.
- **Window to the side** of your screen, not directly behind it or behind you, to avoid glare and squinting.
- **A door or boundary** you can close or signal with, if others share your home.
- **Power outlets nearby**, so you aren't running extension cords across walkways.
- **Reasonable temperature and airflow.** A stuffy, overheated corner is tiring.
- **A background you're comfortable showing** on video calls, or space to add one.

**Free fix:** If you don't have a spare room, a corner of a bedroom or living room works. Face the desk so your back isn't to the main traffic flow, and use a folding screen, bookshelf, or even a plant to mark it off.

## 2. Chair

You'll spend more time in your chair than on any other piece of equipment.

- Feet flat on the floor or a footrest.
- Knees roughly level with or slightly below your hips.
- Two to four fingers of space between the seat edge and the back of your knees.
- Support in the curve of your lower back.
- Armrests low enough that you can pull in close to the desk, with relaxed shoulders.

**Free fixes:** A rolled towel behind your lower back for lumbar support. A firm cushion to raise a low seat. A box or stack of books as a footrest.

**When to buy:** If you work full time from home and your current chair can't be adjusted to meet these points, a proper chair is often the most worthwhile purchase. Look for [[office-chairs|office chairs with adjustable seat depth and lumbar support]].

## 3. Desk

- **Height:** When you sit with relaxed shoulders, your elbows are at about desk height (or the desk is slightly lower).
- **Depth:** Enough room to place your screen about an arm's length away with a keyboard in front.
- **Width:** Room for your keyboard, mouse, and anything else you use daily without constant rearranging.
- **Legroom:** Nothing under the desk blocks your knees or feet.
- **Stable:** No wobble when you type.

**Free fixes:** If the desk is too high, raise your chair and use a footrest. If it's too low, sturdy furniture risers or blocks under the legs can help, as long as the desk stays stable.

**Optional:** If you want to alternate sitting and standing, consider a sit-stand desk or a converter that sits on your current desk.

## 4. Screen

- Top of the screen at or slightly below eye level.
- Screen about an arm's length away.
- Directly in front of you, centered on your keyboard.
- Tilted back slightly.
- Text size comfortable to read without leaning in.

**Laptop users:** A laptop's screen and keyboard can't both be at the right height at once. For daily use, raise the laptop so the screen is at eye level and add a separate keyboard and mouse.

**Free fixes:** Books or a sturdy box as a monitor or laptop riser. Increase display scaling if you catch yourself leaning forward.

**When to buy:** A [[laptop-stands|laptop stand]] plus external keyboard and mouse if you work mainly from a laptop. An external monitor if you spend most of the day squinting at a small screen.

## 5. Keyboard and mouse

- Keyboard at about elbow height, close to your body.
- Keyboard feet folded in, so your wrists stay roughly straight.
- Mouse right next to the keyboard at the same height.
- Wrists floating while typing, resting on palm heels only during pauses.

**Free fixes:** Fold the keyboard feet in. Move the mouse closer. Raise pointer speed a little so you move your wrist less.

## 6. Lighting

- Enough ambient light in the room, especially in the evening.
- No bright window directly behind your screen or reflecting on it.
- Task light for your desk area if the room is dim.
- Your face is evenly lit for video calls, ideally from the front or a front-side angle.

**Free fixes:** Rotate your desk relative to the window. Use blinds or curtains. Put a lamp you already own to the side of your desk, aimed at the work surface rather than the screen.

## 7. Video calls and audio

- Camera at roughly eye level, so you're not filmed from below.
- Light source in front of you, not behind you.
- Microphone that picks up your voice clearly without much room echo.
- Headphones or a headset so others don't hear their own voices echo back.
- Mute and camera controls you can reach quickly.

**Free fixes:** Raise your laptop on books to bring the camera up. Sit facing a window during daytime calls. Soft furnishings like rugs, curtains, and a bookshelf reduce echo. Wired earphones with a built-in mic are often clearer than a laptop mic.

**When to buy:** If you're on calls much of the day, a comfortable [[headsets|headset with a noise-reducing microphone]] is often a bigger improvement than a new camera.

## 8. Tech and connectivity

- Reliable internet with enough speed for video calls. Test it at the times you usually work.
- Router placed centrally and out in the open, or a wired connection to your desk if Wi-Fi is weak.
- Surge protector or power strip for your equipment.
- Backups for important work files.
- A plan for outages: phone hotspot, nearby café, or co-working space.
- Cables tidied so nothing trips you or tugs when you move the desk.

**Free fixes:** Move the router off the floor and away from thick walls or metal. Bundle cables with twist ties or reusable straps.

## 9. Comfort and environment

- Water within reach.
- A comfortable room temperature, with a way to adjust it.
- Noise under control: a door you can close, or headphones.
- A small plant, a picture, or anything that makes the space feel pleasant. It helps more than it seems.

## 10. Daily habits

The best setup still causes trouble if you sit perfectly still in it for ten hours.

- Change position at least every 30 to 60 minutes.
- Look away from the screen at something far away regularly to rest your eyes.
- Get up and walk around a few times a day, even briefly.
- Set a clear start and end to your workday.
- Keep the workspace clear enough that you can start work without tidying first.

## A realistic buying order

If you need to buy, spread it out and start with the items that affect you for the most hours:

| Priority | Item | Buy if... |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adjustable chair | Your current chair can't be set up to the checklist above |
| 2 | External keyboard and mouse | You work mainly on a laptop |
| 3 | Laptop stand or monitor | Your screen is below eye level all day |
| 4 | Headset | You're on calls for hours a day |
| 5 | Task lighting | Your room is dim or you work evenings |
| 6 | Sit-stand desk or converter | You want to alternate sitting and standing |

Accessories like cable trays, mats, and footrests come after these, when you know exactly what's bothering you.

## Common setup mistakes

- **Working from the sofa or bed long term.** Fine occasionally, but it usually leads to slouching and neck strain.
- **Using a dining chair indefinitely.** Hard, flat seats with no back support get uncomfortable over a full workday.
- **Laptop flat on the desk all day.** The screen ends up far too low.
- **Buying gear before adjusting what you have.** Many problems disappear once your chair height and screen height are set.
- **Ignoring discomfort.** Small aches are often a sign something in the setup needs changing.

If you develop pain that persists or gets worse, or numbness or tingling in your hands, arms, or legs, have it checked by a doctor or physiotherapist rather than relying on equipment changes alone.

## Bottom line

Start with location, chair, and screen height; they have the biggest effect. Use free fixes like books, towels, and boxes to test what helps before you buy. Then add equipment one piece at a time, based on what the checklist tells you is still missing.
