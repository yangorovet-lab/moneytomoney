The cleanest way to manage cables under a standing desk is to mount everything to the desk itself: put a cable tray or basket under the back edge, set your power strip inside it, plug all your gear into that strip, and run one bundled drop (the strip cord, the desk motor cord, and a network cable if you use one) down to the wall with enough slack for the desk's highest position. When the desk moves, everything on it moves together, and only that single bundled cable has to flex. The rest is detail: which tray to use, how much slack to leave, and how to keep the dropping cable from snagging.

## Why standing desks need a different approach

On a fixed desk, cables can hang loose and nothing bad happens. A standing desk often travels up and down by 60 cm (about 2 feet) or more, and that changes things:

- **Anything tied to the floor or wall can get pulled tight** when the desk rises, yanking plugs out or pulling a monitor or laptop toward the edge.
- **Loose loops can get pinched** in the frame, crossbar, or legs on the way down.
- **Cables dragging on the floor** get caught by chair wheels and vacuum cleaners.

The goal is simple: everything that needs power lives on the desk, and only one controlled "umbilical" connects the desk to the wall.

## What you need

You can do a basic version with things you already own, then upgrade later.

| Item | What it does | Free or cheap alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Cable tray or basket | Holds the power strip, adapters, and extra cable length under the desk | A sturdy shoebox-size plastic bin screwed under the desk, or a wire shelf basket |
| Power strip with surge protection | Gives every device one plug point that rises with the desk | The strip you already have |
| Hook-and-loop straps | Bundles cables and can be reopened easily | Twist ties from bread bags, or strips cut from an old T-shirt |
| Adhesive cable clips | Guides cables along the underside of the desk | Binder clips screwed or taped in place |
| Cable spine or split sleeve | Keeps the cord to the wall neat and flexible | Several straps spaced every 20 to 30 cm |

Skip zip ties for anything you might change later. They are cheap and neat, but you have to cut them every time you add a device, and it is easy to nick a cable doing it.

## Step-by-step setup

### 1. Take stock and unplug

List everything that needs power or a connection: monitors, laptop charger, lamp, speakers, phone charger, dock, network cable, and the desk motor itself. Unplug it all and clear the surface.

### 2. Measure the full height range

Lower the desk to its lowest setting and measure from the underside of the desktop to the outlet. Then raise it to the highest setting and measure again. The difference is how much slack your one cable to the wall needs. Add about 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 inches) on top of that as a safety margin so nothing is ever pulled tight.

### 3. Mount the tray

Install the tray under the back edge of the desk, centered or toward the side closest to your outlet. A few tips:

- **Check the frame first.** Many standing desks have a crossbar or motor housing under the back. Make sure the tray will not collide with it and that the screws are short enough not to poke through the desktop. Measure the desktop thickness before you drill.
- **Leave space from the back edge** if the desk sits against a wall, so the tray does not scrape the wall when the desk moves.
- **No-drill option:** clamp-on trays attach to the desk edge, which is handy if you rent the desk or have a glass or thin top.

If you are shopping for one, [[cable-trays|look at under-desk cable trays]] that list their width and mounting method clearly. A tray around 60 to 80 cm wide fits a standard power strip plus a few chargers.

### 4. Put the power strip in the tray

Lay or mount a surge-protected power strip in the tray. Pick one with a long enough cord, since that cord becomes your main connection to the wall, and widely spaced outlets if you have bulky chargers.

### 5. Plug everything into the strip

Route each cable from the desktop to the tray by the shortest path, usually over the back edge or through a grommet hole. Coil extra length loosely inside the tray and hold it with a strap. Avoid wrapping cables tightly around the strip or a charger brick.

Keep data and video cables (monitor, USB, dock) on the desk side too. If your computer tower sits on the floor, see the section below.

### 6. Create one tidy drop to the wall

Now bundle the cables that must leave the desk: the power strip cord, the desk motor cord, and a network cable if you use one. Run them together from the tray down one leg or down the back, and then to the outlet.

- **Attach the bundle to the desk frame at the top,** not to the wall, so it moves with the desk.
- **Let the slack form a gentle loop** at the bottom rather than a tight vertical line.
- **Use a cable spine or split sleeve** to keep the bundle neat. A spine is a segmented plastic column that bends as the desk rises and lowers. A split sleeve is a flexible fabric or plastic tube you can open to add or remove cables.

Check your desk's manual on where to plug the motor. Some manufacturers recommend the wall outlet rather than a power strip.

### 7. Test the full range

Run the desk from lowest to highest at least twice while watching the cables. Look for:

- Any cable pulling tight at the top
- Anything catching on the crossbar, legs, or tray on the way down
- Cables touching the floor at the lowest position
- Monitor arm or laptop cables stretching as the desk moves

Fix problems now by adding slack or rerouting.

## Handling the tricky pieces

### Computer tower on the floor

A tower that sits on the floor means monitor, USB, and power cables must travel the full height range. You have three choices:

- **Hang the tower under the desk** with an under-desk mount, so it moves with the desk. This is the cleanest option, but check your desk's weight capacity first.
- **Put it on a small shelf or stand** attached to the desk frame.
- **Leave it on the floor** and use long enough cables with a generous slack loop, bundled in the same spine or sleeve as the power drop.

### Monitor arms

Run monitor cables along the channels built into the arm, leaving a small loop at each joint so it can move freely, then bring them down to the tray. Move the arm through its full range before strapping anything tight.

## Common mistakes

- **Not leaving enough slack.** This is the most common problem. Always measure the full height range first.
- **Taping or clipping the drop cable to the wall.** The cable then pulls when the desk rises.
- **Overloading a power strip,** or plugging one strip into another. Check the strip's rated capacity against what you plug in, and do not chain strips together.
- **Blocking the motor or control box.** Keep cables clear of the motor housing and control panel so nothing gets pinched or overheats.
- **Ignoring sudden stops.** Many desks stop or reverse when they detect resistance. If yours does this, check for a snagging cable.
- **Cables resting on the floor.** Chair wheels roll over them and wear through the insulation over time.

## Quick routing by desk setup

| Setup | Recommended approach |
|---|---|
| Laptop only, one monitor | Small tray or basket, one power strip, one drop |
| Two or three monitors on arms | Wide tray, cables through arm channels, spine for the drop |
| Tower PC | Under-desk tower mount if the weight capacity allows; otherwise generous slack loop |
| Desk against a wall | Tray set back slightly from the edge, drop down the back leg |
| Desk in the middle of a room | Cable spine from the tray to the floor, then a floor cable cover to the outlet |
| Rented or glass-top desk | Clamp-on tray and adhesive clips, no drilling |

## If you are still choosing a desk

If you are still choosing a desk, look for models that include a cable tray or grommet holes. When you [[standing-desks|compare standing desks]], also check the weight capacity if you plan to hang a tower or tray full of chargers underneath.

## Bottom line

Treat the desk as a self-contained unit: tray underneath, power strip in the tray, everything plugged into the strip, and one bundled cable to the wall with enough slack for the tallest setting. Test the full range before you finish.

### Quick checklist

- Measure the height range and add 15 to 20 cm of extra slack
- Mount a tray under the back edge, clear of the frame and motor
- Put a surge-protected power strip in the tray
- Plug every device into the strip, coil extra length loosely
- Bundle the cords to the wall and attach them to the desk frame, not the wall
- Use a cable spine or split sleeve for the drop
- Run the desk up and down twice and fix anything that pulls or snags
