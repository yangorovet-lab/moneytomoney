Improve your video call setup in this order: audio first, then light, then camera angle, then background, then connection. People forgive a slightly soft picture far more easily than muffled or echoing sound, and most camera problems are really lighting problems. Almost everything in that list can be improved for free by moving your laptop, your lamp and yourself. Only after that is it worth buying a headset, a webcam or a light.

## The priority list

| Priority | What to fix | Free fix | Worth buying if needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Audio | Wired earbuds with mic, quieter room, soft furnishings | Headset with a boom mic |
| 2 | Lighting | Face a window, move a lamp behind your screen | Dimmable, color-adjustable lamp |
| 3 | Camera angle | Raise laptop to eye level on books | Laptop stand or external webcam |
| 4 | Framing | Sit back, center yourself | Nothing |
| 5 | Background | Tidy, declutter, add a light behind | Nothing, usually |
| 6 | Connection | Move closer to router, use a cable | Ethernet adapter or cable |

## 1. Audio: the fastest noticeable upgrade

### Why it matters most

Laptop microphones pick up keyboard clicks, fan noise and room echo. Speakers leak sound back into the mic, which calling apps then have to filter out, sometimes cutting off your voice.

### Free fixes

- **Use any wired earbuds with a built-in mic.** They stop speaker echo and put the mic closer to your mouth. Many people already own a pair.
- **Soften the room.** Hard, empty rooms echo. Rugs, curtains, a bookcase, a sofa or even a blanket over a chair behind you reduce echo.
- **Close windows and doors** during calls and move away from appliances.
- **Mute when you're not speaking**, especially if you type while listening.
- **Check your app's audio settings.** Most calling apps have noise suppression you can turn up.

### When to buy

If you're on calls several hours a day, a headset with a boom microphone is the most practical upgrade. The mic sits close to your mouth, so it picks up your voice clearly and less of the room. Look for:

- A flexible boom mic that you can position near the corner of your mouth
- Comfortable padding and a light clamp, since you'll wear it for hours
- A mute button or a flip-to-mute boom
- Wired USB for reliability, or wireless with a long battery life if you move around

You can [[headsets|compare headsets with boom microphones for calls]] and prioritize comfort and mic placement over music features.

## 2. Lighting: fix it before blaming the camera

A grainy, washed-out or dark video is usually a lighting problem.

- **Face the light.** Put your desk so a window is in front of you or to one side, never behind you.
- **Add a lamp behind your screen**, a bit above eye level, pointed toward your face. Diffuse it by bouncing it off a wall or a sheet of white paper.
- **Turn off harsh overhead lights** if they cast shadows under your eyes.
- **Avoid mixing light colors.** Warm lamps and cool daylight together can make your face look two-toned.

A quick test: open your camera app and look at yourself. If your face is darker than the wall behind you, add light in front or reduce light behind.

## 3. Camera angle: bring it to eye level

The classic laptop call view looks up your nose with the ceiling behind you. It happens because the laptop is on the desk and the camera sits below your face.

**Fix it for free:** stack books or a sturdy box under your laptop until the camera is at eye level or slightly above. Then use an external keyboard and mouse if you'll be typing during calls.

**Why it helps beyond looks:** raising the screen also helps your neck. The top of the screen should be at or slightly below eye level for everyday work, which lines up well with a good camera angle.

A [[laptop-stands|height-adjustable laptop stand]] is the tidy, permanent version of the book stack.

### Should you get an external webcam?

Built-in laptop cameras are often acceptable in good light. An external webcam makes sense if:

- You use a separate monitor and the laptop sits off to the side, so you look away from its camera.
- Your built-in camera looks grainy even with good lighting.
- You want to mount the camera on top of your main monitor, so you look toward it naturally.

Features to look for include good low-light performance, autofocus, a wide-enough field of view to fit you comfortably but not your whole room, and a clip that sits securely on your monitor. You can [[webcams|browse webcams that clip to a monitor]] if your laptop camera isn't in the right spot.

## 4. Framing: how you sit in the picture

- **Center yourself** with your eyes about a third of the way down the frame.
- **Show head and shoulders**, with a little space above your head. Not so close that your forehead is cut off, not so far that you're a small figure in a big room.
- **Sit an arm's length or so from the camera.** Too close distorts your face.
- **Look at the camera** when you're speaking to someone directly. Placing the call window near the camera makes this easier.

## 5. Background: calm and a little darker than you

- **Declutter what's in the frame.** Check what the camera actually sees, including the edges.
- **Avoid bright windows or lamps behind you.**
- **Add depth.** Sitting a few feet from the wall looks better than sitting against it. A lamp or a plant behind you adds interest.
- **Use blur or virtual backgrounds sparingly.** They can clip your hair and hands and they work best with good lighting.

## 6. Connection: stable beats fast

Choppy video and robotic audio are usually connection problems, not camera problems.

- **Use a cable** if you can. A wired ethernet connection is almost always more stable than wifi.
- **Move closer to the router**, or remove walls and obstacles between you.
- **Pause big downloads, cloud backups and streaming** on other devices during important calls.
- **Close extra browser tabs and apps** that compete for bandwidth or processing power.
- **Turn off your video briefly** if the connection struggles. Audio is more important.

## A quick pre-call routine

Before any important call, take one minute to check:

1. Headset or earbuds connected and selected as the input and output in the app
2. Camera at eye level and not blocked
3. Face well lit, nothing bright behind you
4. Background clear
5. Notifications off
6. Water nearby, phone silenced

Most calling apps have a preview screen. Use it.

## Keep it comfortable for long days

If you're on calls for hours, your setup has to work for your body, not only for the camera.

- Keep the screen at eye level and about an arm's length away.
- Choose a headset you can wear all day without pressure headaches.
- Stand for some calls if you have a standing desk. Adjust the camera height when you switch.
- Take audio-only calls when video isn't needed, and walk around.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying a webcam before fixing light.** A new camera in a dark room still looks bad.
- **Using laptop speakers and mic in an echoey room.** Earbuds fix this for free.
- **Camera below chin level.** Raise it.
- **Window behind you.** Turn around.
- **Ignoring the connection.** A cable often fixes more than new gear.

## Bottom line

Fix audio first with wired earbuds or a headset, face your light source, raise your camera to eye level, frame yourself head and shoulders, tidy the background, and use a wired connection for important calls. Each step costs little or nothing and makes a bigger difference than any single piece of gear.
