Working from a couch or bed will never be as comfortable over a full day as a proper desk, but you can make it far less punishing. The three changes that matter most: get the screen up toward eye level instead of on your lap, support your back so you're not slumping, and keep your wrists straight with the keyboard low and close. Then keep sessions short and change position often. The setups below show how to do that with pillows and household items first, and a couple of inexpensive accessories if you do it regularly.

## Why couches and beds are hard on your body

Soft furniture works against you in a few specific ways:

- **No firm back support.** Cushions compress, and your pelvis rolls backward, so your lower back rounds into a C shape.
- **Screen too low.** A laptop on your lap or a low coffee table means looking down for long stretches, which loads the neck.
- **Arms unsupported.** With nowhere to rest, your shoulders hunch and your wrists bend up to reach the keyboard.
- **Legs out in front.** Sitting with legs straight on a bed tilts the pelvis back and pulls the lower back into a slump.
- **Heat and airflow.** Laptops on duvets and cushions can block vents and run hot.

None of this is harmful for a short session. Problems tend to start when a couch or bed becomes the default workspace for hours at a time.

## The core principles

Whatever surface you're on, aim for:

1. **Screen high:** top of the screen as close to eye level as you can get, about an arm's length away.
2. **Back supported:** lower back filled in with a cushion, spine upright or slightly reclined.
3. **Wrists straight:** keyboard low and close, elbows around 90 degrees.
4. **Short sessions:** change position at least every 30 minutes and cap total time.

The challenge is that a laptop joins screen and keyboard together, so you can't have both at their ideal height at once. The fixes below are compromises that get you closer.

## Couch setup

### Option 1: Sit upright with the laptop raised

Best for longer sessions on the couch.

- **Sit all the way back** so your hips touch the back of the couch. If the seat is too deep, put one or two firm cushions behind you so you can sit back with your feet on the floor.
- **Add lumbar support.** A small cushion or rolled towel at the small of your back keeps you from slumping.
- **Raise your feet** if they don't reach the floor. A footstool, ottoman, or a few firm books works.
- **Raise the laptop.** Put it on a firm pillow on your lap, a lap desk, or a tray table so the screen is higher and angled toward you.
- **Consider a separate keyboard.** If you work this way often, put the laptop on a coffee table stacked with books at eye height and use a wireless keyboard on a lap tray.

### Option 2: Use the armrest

- Sit sideways with your back against the couch arm, supported by cushions.
- Rest your forearms on a pillow on your lap so your shoulders stay relaxed.
- Keep this for short sessions, since it twists the body more.

### Option 3: Coffee table as desk

Coffee tables are usually far too low for typing, and you end up hunched forward. Only use one if you raise the laptop on books and use an external keyboard on your lap.

## Bed setup

### Option 1: Sit upright against the headboard

This is the most workable bed position.

- **Stack firm pillows** behind your back so you sit fairly upright, not reclined at a steep angle.
- **Put a pillow or rolled towel at your lower back** for lumbar support.
- **Bend your knees** or put a pillow under them. Sitting with legs straight tends to pull your lower back into a slump. Knees slightly bent relaxes the hamstrings and helps keep the pelvis upright.
- **Use a lap desk or bed tray** so the laptop sits on a firm, flat surface, not directly on the duvet. This lifts the screen and keeps vents clear.
- **Tilt the screen back** so you look at it straight on.

### Option 2: Cross-legged with support

- Sit cross-legged with a firm pillow under your hips so your knees are lower than your hips.
- Lean your back against pillows or the headboard.
- Use a tray or pillow to bring the laptop up to a comfortable height.
- Switch leg positions every so often.

### Positions to avoid

- **Lying on your stomach** propped on your elbows. It bends your lower back and neck backward and loads your shoulders.
- **Lying on your side** with the laptop beside you. It twists the neck and spine.
- **Reclining nearly flat** with the laptop on your chest. It pushes your chin toward your chest.

These are fine for a quick message but not for real work.

## Setup comparison

| Setup | Neck | Back | Wrists | Good for |

| Bed, upright against headboard with lap desk | Fair | Good | Fair | Most bed work |
| Bed, cross-legged with tray | Fair | Fair | Fair | Short sessions |

## Accessories that help (if you do this often)

Try free fixes first: firm pillows, a rolled towel, a stack of books, a tray or cutting board as a flat surface. If you regularly work from the couch or bed, a few items make a noticeable difference:

- **Lap desk or bed tray.** A flat, firm surface that lifts the laptop and keeps vents clear. Look for adjustable legs or an adjustable tilt.
- **Wireless keyboard and mouse.** Letting you raise the screen and keep the keyboard low is the biggest upgrade for laptop comfort. [[external-keyboards|Browse compact wireless keyboards]] that fit on a lap tray.
- **Portable laptop stand.** A folding stand on a lap desk or side table can lift the screen higher. [[laptop-stands|Compare portable laptop stands]] if you want something that folds flat.
- **Wedge or backrest pillow** for sitting upright in bed without sliding down.
- **Footrest or ottoman** for the couch if your feet don't reach the floor.

## Time limits and movement

Even the best couch or bed setup is a compromise. Keep it that way:

- **Set a limit.** Many people find an hour or two is plenty before stiffness sets in.
- **Change position every 30 minutes** or so. Switch from couch to bed to a table, or stand for a call.
- **Take short breaks** to stand, stretch your neck and shoulders, and look at something far away to rest your eyes.
- **Do focused work at a table.** Save the couch or bed for email, reading, or calls.

If a desk isn't available, a kitchen or dining table with a firm chair and the laptop raised on books is usually better than a couch for long work sessions.

## Keep work out of bed when you can

Working from bed can blur the line between rest and work. If you have trouble winding down at night, try keeping work to the couch or a table and reserving the bed for sleep.

## When to get help

Occasional stiffness after a couch session is common and usually eases once you move. See a doctor or physiotherapist if you have:

- Neck, back, or wrist pain that lasts more than a few weeks or keeps getting worse.
- Numbness, tingling, or weakness in your arms, hands, or legs.
- Pain that wakes you at night.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Sitting back with firm cushions behind you, not slumped
- [ ] Rolled towel or cushion at your lower back
- [ ] Laptop on a firm surface, raised toward eye level
- [ ] Knees slightly bent in bed, feet supported on the couch
- [ ] Separate keyboard if you do this regularly
- [ ] Position change every 30 minutes
- [ ] Focused, long work moved to a table or desk
