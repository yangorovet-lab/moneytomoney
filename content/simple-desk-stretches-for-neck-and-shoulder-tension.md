Here are nine gentle stretches you can do without leaving your chair: chin tucks, side neck stretches, neck rotations, shoulder rolls, shoulder blade squeezes, a doorway or seated chest stretch, an upper back stretch, a levator scapulae stretch and an overhead side reach. Do two or three of them every hour or so, hold each for about 15 to 30 seconds, and move slowly. Stretching should feel like mild tension, never sharp pain.

Stretching helps most when you pair it with a better setup, so after the routine you'll find the desk fixes that keep tension from building up in the first place.

## Before you start

- **Go slowly.** No bouncing or jerking.
- **Breathe normally.** Holding your breath tends to tighten the very muscles you're trying to relax.
- **Aim for mild tension.** You should feel a stretch, not pain. If something hurts, ease off or skip it.
- **Sit tall.** Scoot slightly forward on your chair, feet flat on the floor, and let your spine lengthen.
- **Stop and get advice** if a stretch causes pain that shoots down your arm, numbness, tingling, dizziness or headache.

## The nine stretches

### 1. Chin tuck

This is the most useful one for people who lean toward their screen.

1. Sit tall and look straight ahead.
2. Gently draw your chin straight back, as if making a double chin. Keep your eyes level; don't tip your head down.
3. Hold for 3 to 5 seconds, then relax.
4. Repeat 8 to 10 times.

You should feel a gentle stretch at the base of your skull and a light effort in the front of your neck.

### 2. Side neck stretch

1. Sit tall and let your right ear drop toward your right shoulder.
2. Keep your left shoulder relaxed and down. For a bit more stretch, hold the edge of your seat with your left hand.
3. Hold for 15 to 30 seconds. Switch sides.

### 3. Neck rotation

1. Sit tall and slowly turn your head to look over your right shoulder, as far as is comfortable.
2. Hold for 10 to 15 seconds, return to center, then turn left.
3. Repeat 3 times each way.

Keep your shoulders facing forward so the movement comes from your neck.

### 4. Shoulder rolls

1. Lift both shoulders toward your ears.
2. Roll them back, squeezing your shoulder blades together, then down and forward in a slow circle.
3. Do 10 rolls backward, then 5 forward.

This is a quick reset you can do during a meeting with your camera off.

### 5. Shoulder blade squeeze

1. Sit tall with arms relaxed at your sides, or bend your elbows to 90 degrees.
2. Draw your shoulder blades back and slightly down, as if trying to hold a pencil between them.
3. Hold for 5 seconds, relax. Repeat 10 times.

Avoid shrugging up while you squeeze.

### 6. Seated chest opener

Tight chest muscles pull your shoulders forward. To stretch them:

1. Sit near the front of your chair and clasp your hands behind your back (or hold the back of the chair).
2. Gently lift your chest and draw your shoulders back.
3. Hold for 15 to 30 seconds.

Alternative: stand in a doorway, place your forearms on the frame at shoulder height, and step one foot forward until you feel a stretch across the front of your chest.

### 7. Upper back stretch

1. Stretch both arms in front of you and clasp your hands.
2. Round your upper back, drop your head slightly, and push your hands away as if hugging a big ball.
3. Hold for 15 to 30 seconds.

You should feel it between your shoulder blades.

### 8. Levator scapulae stretch

This targets the muscle that runs from the side of your neck to the top of your shoulder blade, a common spot for knots.

1. Turn your head about 45 degrees to the right, then tip your nose down toward your right armpit.
2. Place your right hand lightly on the back of your head for a gentle extra pull. Keep your left shoulder down.
3. Hold for 15 to 30 seconds. Switch sides.

Keep the pull very light. Your hand guides; it doesn't yank.

### 9. Overhead reach

1. Sit tall and reach both arms up.
2. Lean gently to the right, feeling a stretch along your left side and shoulder.
3. Hold for 10 to 15 seconds, return to center, then lean left.

## A simple routine to follow

You don't need to do all nine every time. Here's a way to fit them into a normal workday:

| When | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Every 30 to 60 minutes | Chin tucks and shoulder rolls | About 1 minute |
| Mid-morning | Side neck stretch, neck rotation, shoulder blade squeeze | 2 to 3 minutes |
| After lunch | Chest opener, upper back stretch | 2 minutes |
| Mid-afternoon | Levator scapulae stretch, overhead reach | 2 minutes |
| End of the day | Any three you like, plus a short walk | 5 minutes |

A phone reminder or a timer app helps. Tying stretches to things you already do, like waiting for a file to upload or a meeting to start, makes them easier to remember.

## Why desk work tightens your neck and shoulders

A few patterns show up again and again:

- **Forward head posture.** Leaning toward a screen that's too low, too far away or too small adds load to the neck and upper back.
- **Raised shoulders.** A desk or keyboard that's too high makes your shoulders creep up toward your ears.
- **Reaching.** A mouse far to the side, or a keyboard at the far edge of the desk, keeps the shoulder working all day.
- **Stillness.** Muscles held in one position for long periods get tired and stiff, even in a good posture.
- **Stress.** Many people clench their jaw and lift their shoulders when concentrating or on tense calls.

Stretching helps with stillness and tightness. The rest needs setup changes.

## Setup fixes that stop the tension from coming back

Start with free adjustments:

- **Raise the screen.** The top of the monitor should be at or slightly below eye level and about an arm's length away. Books, a box or a ream of paper work fine.
- **Lower the keyboard,** or raise your chair, so your elbows sit around 90 degrees with relaxed shoulders. Add a box or footrest if your feet no longer reach the floor.
- **Bring the mouse close** to the keyboard so your arm stays near your body.
- **Use a headset** instead of cradling a phone between ear and shoulder.
- **Support your back.** A rolled towel behind your lower back helps you sit upright without effort, which takes load off your neck.

If you work on a laptop, the screen is almost always too low. Raising it and adding a separate keyboard is one of the biggest improvements you can make. ## Common mistakes

- **Stretching into pain.** More intensity doesn't mean more benefit.
- **Only stretching when it already hurts.** Short, regular breaks work better than one long session at the end of a bad day.
- **Jutting the chin forward during chin tucks.** The movement is straight back, not down.
- **Shrugging during shoulder squeezes.** Keep shoulders down, away from your ears.
- **Ignoring the setup.** Stretching can't keep up with a screen that's 10 inches too low.

## When to see a professional

These stretches are general information, not treatment. See a doctor or physiotherapist if:

- Pain is severe, persistent or getting worse.
- You have numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or hands.
- Pain follows an injury or fall.
- You have headaches, dizziness or vision changes along with neck pain.

A physiotherapist can also give you exercises tailored to your body, which is useful if the same area keeps flaring up.

## Quick checklist

- Two or three stretches every hour; hold 15 to 30 seconds.
- Chin tucks and shoulder rolls are the easiest daily habits.
- Gentle tension only; no bouncing.
- Screen at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away.
- Elbows around 90 degrees, shoulders relaxed, mouse close.
- Stand up and walk around at least once an hour.
