For a typical single monitor in the 24 to 27 inch range, a desk about 27 to 30 inches deep is comfortable for most people. A 24 inch deep desk can work, but it gets tight with a monitor on its stock stand. Larger screens, such as 32 inch or ultrawide monitors, are more comfortable on desks 30 inches deep or more. The real test isn't the desk alone: you need enough depth to fit your keyboard and forearms in front and still place the screen about an arm's length from your eyes. If your desk is shallow, a monitor arm can recover several inches.

## Why depth matters

Desk depth is the front-to-back measurement. It decides how far your monitor can sit from your eyes. If the desk is too shallow, the screen ends up too close, and you may notice:

- Having to move your head or eyes a lot to see the whole screen.
- Eye fatigue from focusing up close.
- Leaning back or pushing your chair away to get a comfortable view, which puts your keyboard out of reach.
- No room for your forearms in front of the keyboard.

General guidance is to place a monitor about an arm's length away, which works out to around 20 to 30 inches from your eyes for most people. Bigger screens usually feel better at the farther end of that range, and smaller ones at the closer end.

## What takes up desk depth

It helps to break the desk into zones, from front edge to back:

| Zone | Typical depth | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forearm space | 2 to 6 inches | Optional, but comfortable if you rest forearms on the desk |
| Keyboard | 5 to 7 inches | Compact keyboards are shallower |
| Gap behind keyboard | 1 to 4 inches | Room for a notebook or just breathing space |
| Monitor stand base | 7 to 10 inches | Varies a lot; a monitor arm removes this |
| Monitor panel and back | 2 to 3 inches | Plus any clearance from the wall |

These are approximate, and your equipment may differ. Add them up and you can see why a 24 inch desk fills up quickly.

Your eyes also sit a few inches behind the front edge of the desk when you're sitting up with the keyboard close. That distance counts toward the viewing distance, which works in your favor.

## Recommended desk depth by monitor size

These ranges assume the monitor is centered in front of you and you're using a normal keyboard. Treat them as starting points.

| Monitor | Comfortable desk depth | Workable minimum |
|---|---|---|
| Laptop only, or up to 22 inch | 24 inches | 20 inches |
| 24 inch | 24 to 30 inches | 24 inches |
| 27 inch | 27 to 30 inches | 24 inches with a monitor arm |
| 32 inch | 30 inches or more | 27 inches with a monitor arm |
| 34 inch ultrawide | 30 inches or more | 27 inches with a monitor arm |
| Two monitors side by side | 27 to 30 inches | 24 inches with arms |

Higher resolutions with small text may need you to sit a bit closer, while larger text scaling lets you sit farther back.

## How to measure your own need

Rather than relying on general numbers, you can work out what you need in a few minutes.

1. **Sit at your desk with your chair in its normal position** and your keyboard where you'd type.
2. **Stretch your arm straight out** toward where the screen will go. Your fingertips give you a rough idea of arm's length.
3. **Have someone measure** (or use a tape measure yourself) from your eyes to the spot where a comfortable screen would be. Try holding up a piece of paper at that distance and see if you could comfortably read small text on it.
4. **Measure from the front edge of the desk to that spot.** Add the thickness of the monitor and its stand behind that point.
5. **Compare with your desk.** If the total is more than the desk depth, you'll need a monitor arm, a deeper desk, or a layout change.

## Making a shallow desk work

A deeper desk is the obvious solution, but it isn't always possible in a small room. These fixes, starting with the free ones, can help.

### Push the monitor all the way back

Move the stand so the monitor's back is touching or almost touching the wall. Many people leave several inches of unused space behind the screen.

### Use a compact keyboard

A keyboard without a number pad, or a low-profile model, takes up less room front to back. It also lets the mouse sit closer.

### Move the keyboard to the edge

On a shallow desk, keep the keyboard close to the front edge. You lose forearm space, but you gain screen distance. If the edge feels sharp against your wrists, pad it with a folded towel.

### Use a monitor arm

A monitor arm removes the stand's footprint and can position the screen at, or even past, the back edge of the desk. On a shallow desk, this is often the single most effective fix. Look for [[monitor-arms|monitor arms with enough reach for your screen]] and make sure your desk edge is thick and strong enough for the clamp, or has a grommet hole.

### Try a keyboard tray

A keyboard tray slides out below the desktop, moving your keyboard toward you and freeing the top of the desk for the monitor. Check that it leaves enough legroom and doesn't make your armrests collide with the desk.

### Reduce screen size or raise text scaling

If you're working at a small desk and the screen feels too close, a larger text size in your display settings can make a closer screen more comfortable. And a smaller monitor can be the better choice for a small desk, even if a larger one is tempting.

## Standing desks and depth

Many sit-stand desks come in depths from about 24 to 30 inches. The same rules apply, with one extra point: when standing, people often stand a little farther from the desk than they sit. That can help viewing distance but may push the keyboard out of comfortable reach. If you choose a standing desk, a deeper top (around 30 inches) usually gives you more flexibility, especially if you use a larger monitor.

For desk converters on top of an existing desk, check how far the converter's keyboard platform sits from the monitor platform, since that sets your viewing distance when standing.

## Corner and L-shaped desks

Corner desks often have much more depth in the corner itself. Placing the monitor in the corner can give you a lot of viewing distance, even when each arm of the desk is shallow. Just make sure the keyboard area in front of the corner is wide and deep enough for your arms.

## Don't forget height and position

Depth solves distance, but the screen also needs to be at the right height and centered:

- Top of the screen at or slightly below eye level.
- Screen directly in front of you, aligned with your keyboard.
- Tilted back slightly.

A stack of books or a sturdy box is a fine free way to raise a monitor while you work out the rest.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying a large monitor for a shallow desk** without checking the space.
- **Leaving a gap behind the monitor** instead of pushing it to the back.
- **Pushing your chair away** to get distance, then reaching for the keyboard.
- **Ignoring the stand's footprint.** Some stock stands take up a lot of depth.
- **Choosing a narrow but deep desk for two monitors.** Two screens need width too.

## Bottom line

A depth of 27 to 30 inches fits most single-monitor setups comfortably. A 24 inch desk can work with a smaller screen or a monitor arm. Measure your own viewing distance before buying, push the monitor to the back, and consider an arm if your desk is shallow. If you have regular eye strain, headaches, or neck pain even after adjusting your setup, have it checked by an optometrist, doctor, or physiotherapist.
