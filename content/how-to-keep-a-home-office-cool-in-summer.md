The fastest way to cool a home office is to stop heat getting in before you try to remove it. Close blinds or curtains on sun-facing windows before the sun hits them, open windows only when outside air is cooler than inside, use a fan to move air across your body, and switch off or relocate heat-producing gear. If that isn't enough, a portable or window air conditioner sized for the room is the next step. Most home offices get noticeably more comfortable with free changes alone.

## Why home offices get so hot

Home offices tend to be small spare rooms, attics, or corners of bedrooms, and they collect heat from several sources:

- **Sun through windows.** Direct sun on glass is usually the biggest single heat source in a room.
- **Electronics.** A desktop computer, monitors, a laptop charger, a printer, and lighting all give off heat. In a small closed room, that adds up.
- **Your body.** People give off heat too, which matters in a small room with the door shut.
- **Poor airflow.** Closed doors for privacy trap warm air.
- **Upper floors and attics.** Heat rises, and roofs absorb sun.

Tackle these in order: block, vent, reduce, then cool.

## Step 1: Block the sun

### Close blinds early

Close blinds and curtains on sun-facing windows before the sun reaches them, not after the room is already warm. East-facing windows get morning sun, west-facing windows get the hot late afternoon sun.

### Use the right window coverings

| Option | How much it helps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Light-colored or reflective blinds | Good | Reflects sunlight back out |
| Blackout or thermal curtains | Good | Close fully, extend past the frame |
| Dark curtains | Moderate | Absorb heat and radiate some into the room |
| Exterior shading (awnings, shutters, outdoor blinds) | Best | Stops sun before it hits the glass |
| Reflective window film | Good | Check lease and window warranty first |

Free version: pin a light-colored sheet or blanket over the window. It looks improvised, but it works.

### Move your desk out of direct sun

If sun falls on you or your desk, you'll feel much hotter. Move the desk so the window is to the side, which also cuts screen glare.

## Step 2: Use outside air smartly

Ventilation helps only when outside air is cooler than inside.

- **Open windows in the early morning, evening, or at night** when temperatures drop.
- **Close windows and blinds in the morning** before outside air warms up, to trap the cool air.
- **Create cross-ventilation.** Open windows on opposite sides of the home, and leave interior doors open where possible.
- **Use a window fan** to pull cool night air in, or push warm air out of the hottest room.

Check a weather app or a simple indoor-outdoor thermometer so you know when to switch.

## Step 3: Use fans the right way

Fans don't cool the air. They cool you, by moving air across your skin and helping sweat evaporate. That's why they're cheap and effective.

- **Point the fan at you,** not at the ceiling or an empty corner.
- **A desk fan** close by gives more cooling for less energy than a big fan across the room.
- **Ceiling fans** should spin counterclockwise in summer (on most models) to push air down. Check the switch on the fan housing.
- **Turn fans off when you leave the room.** They don't lower the room temperature.

### The ice trick

Put a bowl of ice or a frozen water bottle in front of a fan for a short burst of cooler air. It helps a bit, briefly, and adds some humidity. Good for an afternoon meeting, not a long-term solution.

## Step 4: Reduce heat from equipment

- **Put computers to sleep** during breaks and shut down at the end of the day.
- **Switch off unused monitors.**
- **Unplug chargers** you're not using. Many stay warm while plugged in.
- **Move printers, network gear, or external drives** out of the room if they don't need to be there.
- **Use LED lighting**; older bulbs produce more heat.
- **Lower screen brightness** to match the room.
- **Dust out computer vents and fans** so they work efficiently.

If your desktop computer runs hot all day, it can raise a small room's temperature noticeably. A laptop generally produces less heat.

## Step 5: Cool yourself, not just the room

Your body temperature is what matters for comfort.

- **Dress in light, loose, breathable clothing.**
- **Drink water through the day.**
- **Use a breathable seat.** Mesh office chairs usually feel cooler than solid foam and faux leather. A cotton cover or towel over a hot seat helps.
- **Cool your wrists and neck** with a damp cloth.
- **Stand up sometimes.** Sitting traps heat against the chair. A standing break lets air reach your back.
- **Schedule demanding work** for the cooler morning hours if you can.

## Step 6: If you need an air conditioner

When free fixes aren't enough, choose cooling that suits your room.

| Type | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Window unit | Efficient, cools well, not too loud | Needs a suitable window, may not be allowed in rentals |
| Portable air conditioner | Flexible, fits most rooms | Takes floor space, noisier, needs a window vent hose |
| Mini-split (ductless) | Quiet, efficient, can heat too | Professional installation, highest upfront cost |
| Evaporative cooler | Low energy | Only works well in dry climates, adds humidity |

Tips for choosing:

- **Size it for the room.** Units are rated by cooling capacity. Check the manufacturer's room size guidance, and size up for sunny rooms, top floors, or rooms full of electronics.
- **Check noise levels** if you take calls. Portable units can be loud.
- **Prefer dual-hose portable units** if you choose portable, since single-hose designs can pull warm air into the room from elsewhere.
- **Seal the window kit** around the hose or unit to prevent warm air leaking in.

Set the thermostat to a comfortable level rather than as low as possible. Combining air conditioning with a fan often lets you set a higher temperature and still feel comfortable.

## Humidity matters too

Humid air feels hotter because sweat evaporates more slowly. In humid climates, a dehumidifier or an air conditioner's dry mode can make a room feel much more comfortable at the same temperature. Dehumidifiers do give off some heat, so they work best alongside air conditioning or ventilation.

## Staying comfortable on calls

Heat and noise often collide: open windows let in traffic, fans and portable units add background hum. A [[headsets|headset with a noise-canceling mic]] keeps your calls clear while the fan runs. Place fans behind or beside your microphone rather than blowing into it.

## Heat and health

Working in a very hot room isn't just uncomfortable. Dizziness, headache, nausea, confusion, or unusual fatigue can be signs of heat illness. Move to a cooler place, drink water, and seek medical help if symptoms are severe or don't improve. People with health conditions, older adults, and those taking certain medications may be more sensitive to heat. Ask a doctor if you have concerns.

## Quick checklist

- Blinds closed on sunny windows before the sun reaches them
- Windows open only when it's cooler outside
- Fan pointed at you, turned off when you leave
- Unused electronics switched off, printers and extra gear moved out
- Light clothing, water nearby, breathable chair
- Desk out of direct sun
- Correctly sized air conditioner if free fixes aren't enough
